Every reflection follows Kolb's Experiential Learning Cycle, moving from the context of the experience to what I learnt from it, and finally to how I intend to apply this learning.

01 · About me

About me

I am a final-year Bachelor of Human Resource Management student at the Singapore University of Social Sciences (SUSS), a recipient of the SUSS Scholarship and an IHRP Certified Associate. Before joining SUSS in July 2023, I worked as a Social Media Assistant at The Smart Local. It was there that I first became interested in how people respond to the way information is communicated to them, and this interest eventually led me to Human Resource Management.

My path to university was not a straight one. In 2022, I retook my A-Level examinations as a private candidate, and preparing on my own taught me to plan my time carefully and to ask for help when I needed it. These are habits I still rely on today.

Over the past three years at SUSS, I have had the opportunity to learn beyond the classroom through overseas modules in Shenzhen and Penang, a semester exchange in Aalborg, a service-learning project with seniors in Jelapang, and two HR internships. Looking back, I noticed that I often learnt the most in moments where I had to understand people whose backgrounds, needs or expectations were different from my own.

These experiences have shaped how I see myself as a future HR practitioner. I hope to work in a role where I can support both employees and the organisation, and where understanding people across different cultures and contexts is part of the work. The reflections in this ePortfolio show how my learning has developed towards this goal.

03 · Reflection one

Across Borders

Learning to work across cultures and disciplines

VSK anchorGlobal and Sustainable Citizenship, supported by Communication Theory lensHofstede's cultural dimensions (CDO355) and Allport's contact hypothesis (HRM358)
  • CDO355 · Shenzhen · Dec 2024
  • NIE351 · Penang · May 2025
  • Semester exchange · Aalborg · Jan – Jun 2026

1Context (What · When · Who · Where)

Between December 2024 and June 2026, I had three opportunities to learn outside Singapore. In December 2024, I travelled to Shenzhen for CDO355 Global Business Experiments, where my team and our buddy from Shenzhen University spent a week running business experiments for a local tea shop that was facing low footfall. In May 2025, I went to Penang for NIE351 Interdisciplinary Global Learning, where my group studied how the island's water supply depends on the forests and rivers upstream. From January to June 2026, I studied at Aalborg University in Denmark on a semester exchange and worked on group projects with Danish and international classmates.

Although these three experiences were very different, each of them required me to work with people whose ways of thinking and working were different from my own.

2Learning (Why · How · So What)

Shenzhen

In our CDO355 presentation, my team applied Hofstede's dimension of individualism and collectivism to the tea shop's situation. As China leans towards a more collectivist culture, we recognised that customers would be more likely to rely on recommendations from others. For this reason, we focused our promotions on encouraging customers to return and bring their friends. Not all of our experiments reached the targets we had set, and we had to adjust our approach after each round of testing. Being able to speak Mandarin allowed me to communicate with customers directly. Our buddy also helped us with more technical business terms when we spoke with the store manager. Through this, I realised that sharing a language with someone does not always mean sharing the same context, and that understanding the local way of doing things was just as important.

Penang

In Penang, I faced a similar challenge, but this time it was between disciplines rather than cultures. My groupmates were from the School of Business and were quick to understand the economic implications of Penang's reliance on the Sungai Muda river for most of its water supply. For me, the issue only felt real when a local fisherman shared how changes upstream affected his catch and his livelihood. When we prepared our final presentation, we decided to combine both perspectives by pairing key statistics with scenes from our trip. This experience helped me understand global citizenship as recognising how my own actions are connected to people and systems beyond my immediate surroundings.

Aalborg

In Aalborg, most of my learning took place through group projects with Danish and international students. I enjoyed the flat and open way of working, which was consistent with what Hofstede's framework suggests about Denmark. There were still times when language and cultural differences led to small misunderstandings within our groups. In those moments, we took the time to clarify what each of us meant until we reached a shared understanding. I also noticed how my classmates balanced their studies, work and personal lives, which gave me a different perspective on what a healthy working culture can look like.

Outside of class, I celebrated Chinese New Year with my friends and joined them for a dinner where each of us cooked a dish from our own culture. These moments reminded me of Allport's (1954) contact hypothesis, which I first studied in HRM358. Spending time together on equal terms helped us understand one another far better than classroom discussions alone could. One of my friends had moved to Denmark from Ukraine when he was young, and hearing how grateful he was for his education made me reflect on the opportunities I have in Singapore.

Looking backHofstede's framework gave me a general sense of what to expect in each place and helped me to prepare. However, it was only through working closely with people and getting to know them that I could understand them as individuals. I now see cultural frameworks as a useful starting point, but they cannot replace genuine conversation.

3Future (Now What · Action)

As an HR practitioner, I hope to work with colleagues from many different backgrounds, and these three experiences have shaped how I intend to approach that work.

  1. I will use cultural frameworks to prepare for working with new teams, and take the time to understand each person beyond these general patterns.
  2. I will look for ways to bring colleagues from different backgrounds together through shared projects or buddy arrangements.
  3. I will continue to use both English and Mandarin to support communication between people, especially in regional work settings.

04 · Reflection two

In the Workplace

Developing as an HR practitioner through two internships

VSK anchorCommunication and Leadership Theory lensAssessment and Selection (HRM373), Training and Development (HRM233) and Human Capital Measurement (HRM318)
  • HR Intern · Forvis Mazars · Jun – Dec 2025
  • Global HR Summer Analyst · Bank of America · Jun – Aug 2026

1Context (What · When · Who · Where)

From June to December 2025, I interned with the HR team at Forvis Mazars, where I supported the full recruitment cycle, from screening applications and coordinating interviews to preparing offer letters. I also helped to maintain employee records, draft internal announcements and support HR events. From June to August 2026, I was a Global HR Summer Analyst at Bank of America, where I supported employee enquiries and contributed to HR projects, including resources for new joiners and reporting work.

Across both internships, I often found myself thinking about how HR can work efficiently while still making sure each person feels properly supported.

2Learning (Why · How · So What)

Recruitment and communication

In HRM373 Assessment and Selection, I learnt that a good selection process should be fair, consistent and focused on what the job requires. At Forvis Mazars, I saw how these principles were applied across a large number of applications, especially for internship roles. As I was often the main point of contact for candidates, I also learnt that the way we communicate shapes how candidates experience the organisation. Representing the firm at a university career fair later showed me that recruitment begins well before an application is submitted.

Adapting to feedback

One of my more challenging moments came when I was asked to make several changes to materials for a school learning journey on the day before the event. At the time, I saw the last-minute feedback as a sign that I had not done well enough. With more experience, I came to understand that revisions are a normal part of working with stakeholders. I learnt to prioritise the changes that mattered most to the audience, to check with my supervisor instead of making assumptions, and to adjust my language so that it suited secondary school students.

Working in a larger HR organisation

My second internship took place in a much larger organisation, where HR was made up of many specialist teams working together. Supporting employee enquiries showed me that policies do not always provide a clear answer, and that each case needs to be understood in its own context. When I contributed to resources for new joiners, I drew on what I had learnt in HRM233 Training and Development about how learning needs to be supported after formal training ends. Similarly, my involvement in reporting projects allowed me to apply concepts from HRM318 Human Capital Measurement. Through this, I came to appreciate that data is most useful when it is interpreted with an understanding of the people behind it.

Comparing the two experiences

Comparing the two internships helped me to see HR from different angles. At Forvis Mazars, I worked closely within one HR team and could see how its functions connected from day to day. In my second internship, I saw how different HR teams each play a part in supporting the same employee. To understand this better, I reached out to colleagues across different teams to learn about their work. Both experiences taught me the importance of knowing whom to approach and when to seek guidance.

Looking backBefore these internships, I understood HR mainly through the frameworks I had studied. Through practice, I learnt that these frameworks provide structure, but good HR work also depends on judgement, clear communication and an understanding of each person's situation.

3Future (Now What · Action)

These internships have given me a clearer picture of the kind of HR practitioner I hope to become.

  1. I will apply consistent and fair criteria whenever I am involved in selecting or assessing people.
  2. I will communicate with candidates and employees clearly and promptly, keeping their experience in mind.
  3. I will continue to build my understanding of how HR supports business goals through HRM487 Strategic Human Resource Management and my HRM499 Capstone in January 2027.

05 · Reflection three

In the Community

Helping people feel comfortable taking part

VSK anchorCommunication, supported by Leadership Theory lensService-learning principles and the IAP2 Spectrum (NIE301), with the test-and-learn approach (CDO355)
  • NIE301 · Jelapang · 2025
  • SUSS STARs · Experiential Teambuilding · 2024

1Context (What · When · Who · Where)

In 2025, I took NIE301 Learning with Communities, where my tutorial group worked with an active ageing centre in Jelapang under the theme "Connecting Seniors to Active Ageing". Over the semester, we planned and ran four workshops for the seniors, which were a Zumba session, a paper fan painting session, a Malay dance session for Racial Harmony Day and a Polaroid keychain crafting session. As I had the strongest Mandarin in my group, I usually took on the role of host and translator.

A year earlier, I had served as a facilitator in SUSS STARs, guiding small groups of freshmen through the Experiential Teambuilding programme. Both experiences involved helping people feel comfortable taking part, even though the groups themselves were very different.

2Learning (Why · How · So What)

Listening before planning

During our first site visit, we went door to door with the centre's staff to invite seniors to an upcoming event. Some seniors were keen to join. Others declined, explaining that they had to care for their grandchildren or simply preferred to stay at home. This challenged my assumption that every senior would want to take part in active ageing activities, and it showed me the importance of listening to a community before planning for them. I had a similar realisation as a STARs facilitator when I noticed that some freshmen were quieter during group activities. Instead of assuming they were uninterested, I split the group into smaller discussions, which made it easier for everyone to contribute.

Adjusting along the way

When we ran our workshops, the turnout did not always match our plans. We had hoped to reach younger seniors, but most of the participants were older. We later realised that our weekend sessions may have clashed with the younger seniors' other commitments. Some seniors also told us that they felt the Malay dance session might be too difficult for them. In response, we offered seated movements during Zumba and gave more hands-on help during the crafting session. This reminded me of the test-and-learn approach from CDO355, where feedback from each round helps to improve the next. In our final evaluation, we also reported honestly that we had not reached our intended age group. We hoped this information would help the centre when planning future programmes.

More than translating words

As the host and translator, I learnt that translating for the seniors was not only about the words. I had to slow down, check that everyone understood, and notice when someone needed more help. Our group aimed to work at the "Collaborate" level of the IAP2 Spectrum by asking the seniors for their preferences and feedback throughout the project. It was very meaningful when the seniors told us that they enjoyed the workshops and looked forward to our visits.

Looking backThrough this project, I learnt that a plan is only a starting point. The planning tools we used in NIE301 helped us to set clear goals, but listening to the community and adjusting along the way was what made the workshops meaningful for the seniors.

3Future (Now What · Action)

These experiences have shaped how I hope to approach employee engagement as an HR practitioner.

  1. I will involve employees in shaping engagement activities instead of planning for them without their input.
  2. I will design activities that people with different levels of confidence, ability or language can take part in comfortably.
  3. I will report outcomes honestly, including the areas where things did not go as planned.

06 · Synthesis

How my VSKs connect

Although I have discussed my three VSKs in separate reflections, I found that they developed together and often depended on one another.

Communication and Global Citizenship

My ability to communicate across languages and contexts allowed me to engage more meaningfully with the people I met overseas. Speaking with customers in Mandarin in Shenzhen, combining data with stories in Penang and celebrating Chinese New Year with friends in Aalborg all helped me move from simply observing other cultures to connecting with the people within them.

Leadership and Communication

Taking on leadership roles made me more aware of how I communicate. As a STARs facilitator, I had to find ways for quieter freshmen to contribute. At The Smart Local, when my supervisor was away for over a week, I had to manage our tasks and keep the people we worked with updated. During my internships, I also took the initiative to reach out to colleagues to better understand their work.

Global Citizenship and Leadership

Working in groups in Aalborg, where everyone was expected to contribute equally, showed me that leadership does not always come from having a formal position. It can also come from listening, supporting others and making sure that everyone has the chance to be heard. I saw this again when working with the seniors in Jelapang.

Purpose and agency

Looking ahead

Through my studies and experiences at SUSS, I have come to see HR as work that involves understanding people as much as it involves processes and policies. I hope to become an HR practitioner who can support employees from different backgrounds and help organisations make decisions that are fair and considerate. Receiving the SUSS Scholarship in August 2025 has also given me a greater sense of responsibility to keep learning and to contribute to the communities I am part of. In my final year, I will continue to build on this through HRM487 Strategic Human Resource Management and my HRM499 Capstone.
